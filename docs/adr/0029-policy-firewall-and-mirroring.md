# ADR-0029: One stateful policy layer per VRF, compiled from tags, and Mirrors over VXLAN

**Status:** Accepted (2026-10-20, from the SDN spec #176 that Hilmi filed; recorded by SDN-1, #177). Uses the policy engine of ADR-0019 and ADR-0022 and scopes it to VRFs (ADR-0025).

## Context

ADR-0019 and ADR-0022 define a stateful, identity-based policy engine in the node: rules ordered by priority, one evaluation per flow on ingress, an established-flow fast path, compiled tables swapped atomically, and a conntrack table bounded per network. With VRFs, route leaking and a controller, the open points were: where rules come from, what the default is, how one VRF is kept from exhausting shared state, how domain names are matched, and how traffic reaches an IDS or a sensor without a built-in one.

## Decision

1. **One stateful policy layer, in the node, for all overlay traffic.** It is the ADR-0019/ADR-0022 engine, fed with rules that the controller compiles from tags. There is no separate stateless ACL layer, so there is one place to look when traffic is blocked. WFP still only protects the host (ADR-0022).
2. **Rules are written between tags, or FQDN sets, not IP addresses**, with protocol and port, priority and action, so rules follow identities when addresses change.
3. **Default deny with explicit allow rules.** The engine itself is default deny; the simple case of a first install works through an explicit starter rule, "allow all inside VRF default" (ADR-0034).
4. **Stateful enforcement:** one evaluation per flow on ingress and an established-flow fast path, so replies to allowed flows pass without mirror rules.
5. **The conntrack table has a quota per VRF**, so one busy or misbehaving VRF cannot exhaust the table for the others. Per-protocol conntrack timeouts are part of the VRF in the intent, with sane defaults.
6. **Policy applies to leaked traffic** (ADR-0025): a leak is reachability, not a firewall bypass.
7. **FQDN egress rules** (for example `*.microsoft.com`) are compiled into address sets from the answers that the node's own stub resolver sees (ADR-0028), so cloud services are allowed without chasing their IP ranges.
8. **Inline inspection uses an external appliance** (Suricata, pfSense, Fortinet) placed through hub-and-spoke (ADR-0025). Ranz builds no IDS, IPS or WAF of its own.
9. **A Mirror copies matching traffic to a sensor over VXLAN.** It has a source selector (tag, VRF or node), a filter (protocol or port), a sensor IP and a rate limit, so Zeek or Suricata see the traffic passively and a Mirror never eats the link. The VXLAN encapsulation module is the one the EVPN gateway uses (ADR-0032).

## Consequences

- ADR-0019 decision 3 allows "a default action per network (allow or deny)". Under this ADR the engine's default is always deny, and the allow case is the explicit starter rule. *(ADR-0019 is amended to match, amendment of 2026-10-21.)* The per-network conntrack bound of ADR-0022 becomes a quota per VRF.
- The vpntest harness (seam 2 of #45) checks policy from tags with conntrack, per-VRF conntrack quotas, FQDN rules and mirroring against golden output; the controller conformance seam (ADR-0026) checks the rules each simulated node receives.
- Tickets: SDN-8 (#184), SDN-21 (#197), SDN-23 (#199).

## Amendment 2026-10-25: the compiled rules and their enforcement (SDN-8)

Recorded by SDN-8 (#184), which compiles the Policy resources into the policy engine of CORE-S4-2 (#75). It makes decisions 2 to 6 concrete.

- **A Policy has two ends, a protocol, ports and a priority.** An end (`from`, the end that opens a flow, and `to`) names tags and addresses: a Node with one of its tags, any Node without one, whose address is in one of its prefixes, any address without one. The protocol is `tcp`, `udp`, `icmp` (ICMP, and ICMPv6 in IPv6) or a number from 1 to 255; the ports, destination ports of TCP or UDP, are ports or ranges `first-last`. A Policy holds 16 values of each kind at most. The rules of a VRF judge the first packet of a flow by priority, the lowest first (1000 when a Policy names none, at most 1000000), a deny rule before an allow rule of the same priority, then by name.
- **A VRF declares its conntrack**: `conntrack.maxFlows`, the quota of its table on each Node (65536 by default, at most 1048576), and `conntrack.timeouts` in seconds, per protocol and state (`tcpEstablished` 3600, `tcpTransitory` 120, `udp` 60, `icmp` 30 and `other` 60 by default, at most a week). The defaults are the Node's: the controller sends what the intent declares.
- **The control protocol** carries each rule as a `Rule` object, with its VRF; each `Vrf` carries its policy (`Vrf.policy`: the quota and the timeouts) and each `Peer` its tags. A Node gets the rules of its VRFs whose from or to end can be the Node: every flow that a Node judges has one end at the Node, so a rule whose two ends name only tags that the Node does not have never matches there.
- **In the node** (`src/Cedar/NodePolicy.c`) the engine runs one Network per VRF, named after the VRF, with the quota and the timeouts of the VRF; its members in each VRF are the host with the tags of the Node, each peer in the VRF with its tags, and the end outside the VRF, which has no tag (a peer that a leaked route leads to). Both ends of a flow judge it, the sender on the way out after the 1:1 NAT rules and the receiver on the way in before them, so a Node never relies on the rules of another; the replies of an allowed flow pass on the conntrack fast path at both ends. The document of rules of each Generation goes in force before its forwarding tables, and a member stays while a table holds it, so the flows of a peer that keeps its VRFs and tags survive a Generation and the new rules judge them again on their next packet.
- **Default deny holds for every VRF that has its policy**, which the controller sends with every VRF. A VRF of a state without it, as the vpntest harness builds them for tests of other features, forwards as the starter rule does. When the engine refuses the document of a Generation, the Node drops every packet of those VRFs until a Generation whose document it takes.
- **Next:** users and groups (SDN-31) add identities to the members and the ends; the L2 protections put the guard of the engine on the members of an L2 Network; the access-list fields extend the ends and the match.

## Amendment 2026-10-27: two Devices of one gateway (SDN-80)

Recorded by SDN-80 (#375), the tracer of the WireGuard Devices of the spec #374. The engine judges a Device as an end of its own, by its addresses and its tags. Decision 1 holds for every packet of a Device that reaches a node: a packet to an overlay host, to a Node or to a Device of another gateway passes the engine of its gateway on the way out and the engine of the node at the other end on the way in, protocol and ports included. One kind of packet never reaches a node, and this amendment says what stands in for the engine there.

- **A packet between two Devices of one gateway never reaches the node.** The gateway terminates WireGuard with WireGuardNT (spec #374), and the addresses of its Devices are routes through its WireGuard interface, which they must be for the packets of the overlay to reach them. Windows chooses the next hop of a forwarded packet by its destination alone, so a packet of one Device to another Device of the same gateway leaves on the interface that it came in on, and no route can take it through the adapter of the VRF first and back to the WireGuard interface after. Only a component in the kernel could hand such a packet to the node, and ADR-0009 allows no kernel code of our own.
- **So the gateway keeps them apart with WFP, the one place where WFP does more than protect the host.** Filters at the IP forward layers (`NodeWireGuardFilter.c`, in a dynamic session that each state renews) block a packet that came in on the WireGuard interface and would leave on it, and permit it between two Devices of one VRF whose every packet, in either direction, the rules of the state let through, in the order of the engine. The starter rule does. The filters of the forward layers match the interfaces and the addresses of a packet, and neither its protocol nor its ports ([the filtering conditions available at each filtering layer](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/fwp/filtering-conditions-available-at-each-filtering-layer)), so a rule that allows or denies only some flows between two Devices of one gateway, or only one direction of them, keeps the pair apart whole. The filters only take reach away: a Device never reaches a Device that the policy does not allow it to reach, which is what SDN-80 asks, and it reaches a Device of its own gateway only where the policy allows everything between the two.
- **Devices of two gateways meet in the overlay**, where the engine of each gateway judges them in full. The WireGuard Device tracer shows both on one runner: two Devices of one gateway that the starter rule lets meet, and that a rule that denies the ICMP of one of them keeps apart whole, and a Device of each gateway, between which the same rule blocks the ICMP and lets TCP through. The routes of the second gateway to its own Device are taken away there, so that the runner forwards as two hosts would.
- **Lifting the limit** needs the packets between two Devices of one gateway in user mode: a user-mode WireGuard on the gateway in place of WireGuardNT, whose packets the node would read from the adapter of the VRF, or a kernel component that hands forwarded packets to the node. Each changes a decision (spec #374, ADR-0009) and is the maintainer's to take.

## Amendment 2026-10-29: the Hub node decides the traffic between two Spokes (SDN-19-R2)

Recorded by SDN-19-R2 (#508), from the review of SDN-19 (#195), whose Hub node sends the packet of one Spoke of a VRF of topology hub-and-spoke on to another (ADR-0025 decision 11; decision 8). A Spoke sees the Hub node as the other end of such a packet, so the usual rules "branch to hub: allow" and "hub to branch: allow" would let every Spoke reach every other at both of their ends, though no rule allows it. The first point below closes that; the other two change, for the Hub node, two points of the amendment of SDN-8: which rules a Node gets, and that a Node never relies on the rules of another.

- **The Hub node is the place where the policy of the VRF decides the traffic between two Spokes.** Only it sees both real ends, as both are its peers. Before it sends a packet of one Spoke on to another, it judges it with the same engine and the same rules as a packet of its host, with the Peer that sent it as the `from` end and the Peer of its next hop as the `to` end (`NodePolicyTransit`). With no rule between two Spokes, the default deny (decision 3, ADR-0034) keeps them apart, whatever the rules between the Spokes and the Hub node allow; the replies of an allowed flow pass on the conntrack fast path of the Hub node. A packet that it drops counts with the other drops of its policy, and a Mirror of the VRF at the Hub node copies only what passed.
- **The Hub node gets every rule of its VRF**, the rules between two Spokes among them, whose ends name no tag of its own: it judges flows with neither end at it. Every other Node still gets the rules whose from or to end can be the Node.
- **The destination Spoke relies on the Hub node.** It keeps judging the packet as a packet of the Hub node, the trust anchor of the topology, whose policy the packet passed, as a Spoke cannot tell the real source from the Hub node. For this traffic one Node does rely on the rules of another. A compromised Hub node is out of scope, as it is for every hub.
